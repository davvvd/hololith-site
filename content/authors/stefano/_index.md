bio: 
Professor Stefano Luigi Oscurato is a physicist specializing in photonics, light–matter interaction, and holographic surface structuring. His research centers on azobenzene-based photoresponsive polymers, reconfigurable diffractive optics, holography, and structured light.
He leads the Holo-Lithography Research Group at the Department of Physics “Ettore Pancini” of the University of Naples Federico II, where his team develops holographic and laser-based methods for polymer surface micro/nanostructuring, reconfigurable diffractive micro-optics, and hybrid imaging systems in collaboration with international partners across materials science, optical characterization, computational imaging, and meta-optics.

He currently leads the ERC Starting Grant project HyperMaSH and will coordinate the EIC Pathfinder Open project HoloMorPh from April 2026. Longer bio # <--------------
Professor Stefano Luigi Oscurato is a physicist working in photonics, light–matter interaction, and holographic surface structuring. His research focuses on azobenzene-based photoresponsive polymers, reconfigurable diffractive optics, holography, and structured light.
He leads the Holo-Lithography Research Group at the Department of Physics “Ettore Pancini” of the University of Naples Federico II. The group develops holographic and laser-based approaches for the micro- and nanostructuring of polymer surfaces, reconfigurable diffractive micro-optical elements, and hybrid imaging systems, supported by a broad international network of collaborations spanning materials science, optical and spectroscopic characterization, computational imaging, and meta-optics. 
Prof. Oscurato has served as Principal Investigator in several research projects funded by the Italian Ministry of University and Research. He is currently leading the ERC Starting Grant project HyperMaSH, launched in December 2024, and will coordinate the EIC Pathfinder Open project HoloMorPh, starting in April 2026.
He has delivered numerous invited and contributed talks at leading international conferences, with a particular focus on light-induced surface structuring and holographic vectorial morphing of azomaterial surfaces for reprogrammable diffractive optics. He has also organized specialized conference sessions on photodeformable polymer films and optical materials for structured light. In addition, he serves as Associate Editor of Light: Advanced Manufacturing and as a member of the Early Career Advisory Board of RSC Applied Interfaces.
